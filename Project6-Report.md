In part 1, I had to create a risk map. This section focused on predicting where people
have the highest risk of becoming homeless, and using this information to prevent
homelessness in those areas. The technique used was creating a worst-case scenario
census tract that we would compare this target tract with the rest of the county as if it’s the
most extreme case possible. Using the Similarity Search tool helped compare every tract to
that worse case tract. Those most similar tracts were identified as tracts with higher risk to
generating more homelessness, and then I used graduated symbols to visualize these
tracks based on their risk level. Also making sure tracts with smaller populations were
made more transparent on the map so the areas with larger populations stood out more.
These techniques map out multiple risk factors to homelessness and helps in identifying
which areas are most at risk for future homelessness.

In part 2, I had to prioritize prevention in the maps made. This section focused on finding
the high-risk areas for homelessness as a tool for finding which tracts will benefit most
from preventative programs. Some techniques used were the Similarity Search to find
census tracts who were most alike to the worst-case tract that I chose. This tool clusters
the groups of tracts based on their shared characteristics. This helps to identify the tracts
or general areas that are most at risk. Using this technique this way will help make sure
homelessness prevention programs are done in the places that need it most. I also
symbolized the results using graduated symbols so it’s easier to visualize which specific
tracts are higher risk than others.

In part 3, I had to map homeless communities. This part focused on understanding the
common characteristics within the homeless population. This helped identify patterns in
homelessness which help in determining what kind of support is most helpful in diLerent
homeless communities. The techniques used here were the Hot Spot Analysis tool to find
high density areas of homelessness. This tool is better than just looking at the plain number
from the dataset of count of homelessness in each tract as the proportion of homeless to
non-homeless would ruin the idea of hotspots. This tool shows the true hot spots of
homelessness, and isn’t skewed from larger tracts with bigger populations. I also used
scatter plots and bar charts to compare variables related to homelessness in diLerent
tracts. Comparing these variables in diLerent tracts can help in knowing what issues are
contributing or causing homelessness.

In part 4, I had to promote consensus. This part focused on how to spread resources
related to homelessness. The technique used here was creating diLerent approaches to
choosing the best location for resource allocation. There was the social equity approach,
accessibility approach, high-risk approach, centralized services approach, and street
strategy approach. I marked tracts that fit the threshold for help needed for each of these
approaches, and compiled all those maps to find the tracts that fit multiple of those
approaches. This way, the tracts that fit the needs or multiple goals will be highlighted as
the next best locations for resource allocation. The top priority tracts will be easily shown
for policymakers or homeless programs to focus their eLorts on.

After all parts of the exercise, I learned how to run analysis to predicting homelessness,
cluster/similarity searches, charting data trends, and multi-dimensional analysis of
approaches. Using the techniques learned, I was able to map the distribution and spatial
patterns to homelessness. By looking at the factors that contribute to homelessness and
the characteristics of homeless populations, I could map the best locations for homeless
programs and resources. The project taught me to look at the data with a preventative
approach and how important it is in preventing homelessness for the future.
