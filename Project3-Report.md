# Kent Modeling Incident Density Report
Rahel Worku

Fire and emergency medical service providers can benefit from analyzing the spatial data
of emergency response data to help optimize resources management. Below are the
models of kernel density of fire and emergency medical services that occurred in Kent, WA
with the incidents being split into 5 categories of all responses, fire explosion hazmat,
structure fires, rescue EMS, and service call other. The distribution of incidents is shown
through density maps and contour intervals which can be used for future emergency
service planning. Using the incident response data, I used the kernel density tool to
generate density raster layers for each incident type over a specific radius. I then added
contour lines with intervals defined for each incident type.

Figure 1 of all responses show high clusters of incidents near major roads and in high
population zones. This would show a high demand for fire and EMS responders in those
areas compared to the outskirts of less dense areas. Figure 2 shows the incidents of fire,
explosion, and hazardous responses and it can be seen that data is clustered in similar
areas of central Kent. Figure 3 are one of the more closely clustered type of incident of
structure fires with those incidents happening close together. It could be more likely to be
in areas of heavy infrastructure or commercial buildings. Figure 4 shows the higher density
of rescue and EMS reports in central Kent while also having wide spanning contour lines
across the whole city. This makes sense since rescue services are a commonly used
services called by most of the population so it would be more spread throughout. Figure 5
shows service calls being more distributed across Kent with higher densities near urban
areas of Central Kent.

Using kernel density maps along with contour lines are beneficial in visualizing the
distribution of incident reports. This can be used by departments to map out clear hotspots
for incidents and for future planning of where to guide resources to where it seems to be
necessary most.
