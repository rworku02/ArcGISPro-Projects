# Examining Racial Disparities in Pedestrian Police Stops


This project investigated racial disparities in pedestrian police stops in San Antonio, TX, and New York City, NY. Using ArcGIS Pro, the workflow incorporated disparity index calculations, colocation analysis, and spatial aggregation to identify areas where stops were disproportionately applied to specific racial groups. The analysis also examined whether pedestrian stops were correlated with violent crime or occurred independently of crime hotspots.


The project aimed to develop practical skills in spatial analysis, statistical aggregation, and equity-focused GIS investigations. By linking demographic and policing data, I gained experience in calculating disparity indices, performing colocation analysis, and visualizing patterns of social and racial inequity in urban settings.

## Skills Gained

- Calculating racial disparity indices using census and police stop data

- Conducting colocation analysis to compare stops with violent crime locations

- Performing spatial joins and summarizations to aggregate point data to census tracts

- Visualizing complex demographic and crime data using diverging color schemes

- Interpreting spatial and temporal patterns in policing data to identify inequities

- Generating maps and statistics suitable for policy, research, and advocacy purposes

## Tools & Data

- ArcGIS Pro: Summarize Within, Join Field, Attribute Table calculations, Symbology, Colocation Analysis

- Data Sources: San Antonio pedestrian stop records, NYC pedestrian stop and violent crime datasets, Census population data

- Data Management: Geodatabases for organized storage, spatial aggregation, and analysis

## Key Insights

Identified that Black residents in San Antonio were disproportionately stopped relative to their population in most tracts.
Found that NYC pedestrian stops often occurred isolated from violent crimes, indicating stops not always linked to crime patterns.
Highlighted spatial disparities across census tracts, revealing neighborhoods where stops disproportionately affected minority populations.
Visualizations effectively communicated patterns of inequity and areas requiring potential policy attention or further investigation.
Demonstrated the use of GIS to support data-driven insights for social justice, urban planning, and law enforcement policy evaluation.
