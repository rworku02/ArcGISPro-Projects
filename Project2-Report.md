Food Lifeline should try to help resolve the issue of hunger and food insecurity by
referencing the maps below to get an idea of where to allocate resources. This report will
focus on King County where census tracts with populations below 150% of the poverty
level. The goal of this report and its maps are to help with resource planning so that this
program can implement the proper solutions to the places that need help the most.
It is important to map poverty and its related metrics when it comes to identifying
geographic disparities in food insecurity. Figure 1 below shows the estimates for 2018 to
2022 of assessing the population below 150% of the poverty level, along with the relative
sampling error. Figure 1 shows that areas with the highest poverty counts are concentrated
in urban areas where its likely to be a higher population density. Areas like South Seattle
seem to have higher numbers of people in poverty compared to the more distant rural
areas. The right map in figure 1 shows the level of uncertainty with the mentioned
estimates, and there’s a pattern of high uncertainty in rural or sparsely populated areas
compared to the lower uncertainty in urban areas.

Figure 2 below shows the estimated percentage of the population below 150% of the
poverty level, along with the relative level of uncertainty of those calculated percents. The
higher percentages of those below that poverty level are concentrated in census tracts
located in urban areas such as south King County and southern parts of Seattle. There
seems to be a level amount of uncertainty in those measurements with most of the tracts
with higher percentages also having medium uncertainty. There are very few tracts where
the percentage is medium-high while having a high level of uncertainty. Because of this,
you can still take those percentages at face value knowing their accuracy. Urban areas
having higher percentages of poverty could be attributed to the higher population density
but could also be because of the higher cost of living. There could also be the issue of
socioeconomic disparities due to certain demographics within urban areas having a harder
time with cost of living compared to those living in more rural areas.

Based on these two figures, it would be recommended to focus resources on tracts with
higher percentages and low CV values to address the neighborhoods who need help the
most. Areas where CV values are high could be areas where there needs to be more data
collection or investigating as to why those areas are doing more well oX than others. To
address food insecurity eXectively, Food Lifeline should prioritize resources to census
tracts with high counts and percentages of populations below 150% of the poverty level
and making sure those counts are reliable by checking for low CV values. These figures and
measurements will help Food Lifeline to target its eXorts and programs to where they are
most needed and will make sure they have the biggest impact they could in King County.
