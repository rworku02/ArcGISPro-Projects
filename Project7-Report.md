Analyzing the density of violent crimes in Chicago were most informative for
understanding which areas have the most occurrences of crimes. Using hot spot analysis
helped to spatiality visualize which areas in Chicago had significant amounts of violent
crimes. We had to use a fishnet grid to define the areas to be visualized, and this helped to point out patterns in the data. Also, cross intersecting areas of violent crime with liquor vendors and poverty were useful in showing how other factors play into violent crime rates within certain areas. This could be used in proving how or why violent crimes are more prevalent in some areas more than others and could be a good starting point to trying to decrease that.

The scale that we apply the fishnet polygon grid is important to analyzing our data.
Increasing the grid size fourfold would lead to more generalized crime patterns over a larger area. This could end up hiding smaller complex patterns in the data if more of the data is generalized together. I think generally, it would not be more beneficial to increase grid size largely because it could hide important patterns that you could be missing out on. It would be important to find a balanced grid size that would still point out smaller patterns here and there in the data if you want to uncover important details.

To enhance this study, I would recommend adding more demographic features tied to
causes of poverty. Things like socioeconomic levels, education levels, or housing types
could be other factors that play a part in studying violent crimes. It would be beneficial to find whether other features have a role in certain areas having more violent crimes and would help policy makers with implementing solutions related to those features. This would also help in fixing the root of the problem rather than just dealing with the aftermath of violent crimes since it would be possible to create solutions that can solve the contributing factors.
