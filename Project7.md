# Analyzing Violent Crime in Chicago: Hot Spot and Space-Time Trend Analysis


This project investigated spatial and temporal patterns of violent crime in Chicago, focusing on the relationship between crime, socioeconomic factors, and liquor vendor density. Using ArcGIS Pro, the workflow incorporated hot spot analysis, space-time cube analysis, and overlay techniques to identify areas where crime reduction interventions, such as liquor moratoriums, could be most effective.


The project aimed to develop practical skills in advanced spatial analysis and temporal trend visualization within ArcGIS. By executing a real-world workflow linking crime data, poverty, and liquor vendor locations, I gained experience in hot spot mapping, space-time modeling, and geospatial decision support for public safety initiatives.

## Skills Gained

- Conducting Optimized Hot Spot Analysis for point and polygon data

- Creating space-time cubes to analyze temporal trends in crime

- Performing overlay and intersection analyses to identify overlapping risk areas

- Integrating socioeconomic data (poverty, unemployment) with spatial crime patterns

- Applying geoprocessing tools to support urban planning and policy interventions

- 3D visualization of spatial-temporal data for enhanced interpretability

## Tools & Data

- ArcGIS Pro: Optimized Hot Spot Analysis, Create Space-Time Cube, Emerging Hot Spot Analysis, Intersect, Select by Location/Attribute, 3D visualization

- Data Sources: Chicago Violent Crime 2014, Liquor Vendors, Poverty and Unemployment datasets, Public High School locations

- Data Management: Geodatabases for structured storage and analysis

## Key Insights

Identified areas where violent crime, poverty, and liquor vendor density overlapped, suggesting optimal zones for intervention strategies such as liquor moratoriums.
Detected temporal crime trends using space-time cubes, categorizing locations as new, intensifying, or persistent hot spots over weekly intervals.
Mapped overlapping unemployment and violent crime trends to highlight neighborhoods at elevated socioeconomic and public safety risk.
Selected public high schools within a quarter mile of high-risk zones to inform targeted community engagement and resource allocation strategies.
Produced final maps and 3D visualizations that translate complex spatial-temporal data into actionable insights for policymakers and urban planners.
