# Assessing Access to Public Transit in Seattle


This project evaluated public transit accessibility in Seattle by combining GTFS transit stop data, network analysis, and demographic data from the U.S. Census. Using ArcGIS Pro, I mapped 10-minute walk service areas around transit stops and compared them with block group data on population, poverty, and households without vehicles to identify underserved communities.


The goal was to gain experience applying network analysis for service accessibility and to explore how transit availability intersects with social equity indicators. The project also developed skills in both quantitative analysis (summary statistics of access gaps) and public communication (a press release with maps and calls to action).

## Skills Gained

- GTFS data integration: converting stops into spatial features for analysis

- Network Analyst workflows: service area modeling using 10-minute walk times

- Equity mapping: combining transit service areas with ACS demographic variables

- Spatial statistics: calculating accessibility shares for vulnerable populations

- Communication of findings: designing maps and summaries to inform policy and public awareness

## Tools & Data

- ArcGIS Pro (Network Analyst, Service Area, Clip tools)

- Puget Sound GTFS transit feed (Sound Transit Open Data)

- ACS 2023 5-Year Estimates (population, poverty, vehicle access by block group)

## Key Insights

Most Seattle residents live within a 10-minute walk of transit, but significant gaps remain in low-income and no-vehicle households, who are the most dependent on transit.
Downtown and central neighborhoods show high accessibility, while outlying residential areas contain clusters of underserved populations.
Service area masking revealed locations where demographic need is high but current transit stops are lacking, suggesting priority areas for future expansion.
Summary statistics quantified access inequities, reinforcing the value of combining spatial analysis with demographic context in transit planning.
