# Seattle Health Equity Index Construction and Analysis

This project examined patterns of health equity across Seattle by designing and interpreting a Health Equity Index at the census tract level. Using health and demographic data from the Seattle Racial and Social Equity Index and the U.S. Census Bureau, the project focused on how chronic illness, access to healthcare, and insurance coverage overlap spatially to shape community-level disadvantage.

The primary objective was to gain experience in composite index construction and spatial equity analysis within ArcGIS Pro. The project involved manually calculating a health equity index, reproducing the workflow with ArcGIS’s Calculate Composite Index tool, and comparing weighted versus unweighted results. The analysis culminated in a written report simulating work for a Seattle nonprofit, translating GIS findings into actionable insights for addressing health disparities.

## Skills Gained

Standardization of variables using z-scores and index construction in ArcGIS

Implementation of composite index workflows (manual vs. automated with ArcGIS toolbox)

Application of weighted index analysis to highlight policy-relevant factors (e.g., health insurance coverage)

Thematic mapping and classification using quintiles to visualize spatial patterns

Interpretation of urban health geographies and translation of results into equity-driven reports

## Tools & Data

ArcGIS Pro: data standardization, composite index creation, symbology, map layouts

Seattle Open Data Portal: health metrics from the Racial and Social Equity Index

U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2017–2021: health insurance coverage data

Microsoft Excel: data preparation and correlation testing

## Key Insights

Areas with the highest health disadvantage clustered in South Seattle, where multiple health burdens overlapped.

Incorporating health insurance coverage as a weighted factor significantly shifted the index, emphasizing its importance in evaluating equity priorities.

The comparison between individual health variables and the composite index revealed both redundancy and unique geographic patterns, underscoring the value of a multi-variable approach.

Produced a series of maps and a nonprofit-oriented report that identified priority neighborhoods for targeted health interventions.
