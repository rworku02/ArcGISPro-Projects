# Modeling Fire & EMS Incident Density with Kernel Density and Contours (Kent, WA)


This project modeled the spatial distribution of fire and emergency medical incidents across the Kent Fire Department’s service area in 2016. Using ArcGIS Pro’s Spatial Analyst toolbox, Kernel Density analysis was applied to five categories of emergency response incidents (all responses, fire/hazmat, structure fires, EMS/rescue, and service calls). The resulting density rasters were enhanced with custom contour lines, producing a set of visualizations that reveal where demand for fire and EMS services is concentrated.


The goal of this project was to develop hands-on experience with spatial density modeling and advanced cartographic techniques in ArcGIS Pro. Specifically, the project:

- Applied Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to incident-level response data.

- Modeled spatial variation across five categories of incidents using NFIRS codes.

- Created customized contour lines to highlight gradients of incident density.

- Designed multiple layouts to communicate findings for each incident group.

- Reflected on the usefulness of density and contour mapping for emergency service planning.

## Skills Gained

- Spatial Analyst tools: Kernel Density geoprocessing, Contour with Barriers

- Advanced geoprocessing environments: workspace setup, coordinate system transformation, cell size configuration

- Cartographic visualization: transparency, contour symbology, incident grid overlays

- Multi-layout management: exporting multiple map products for comparative analysis

- Analytical reporting: interpreting patterns of fire, EMS, and service calls for operational planning

## Tools & Data

ArcGIS Pro with Spatial Analyst Extension

Kent Fire Department (synthetic 2016 incident dataset, subset of NFIRS codes)

National Fire Incident Reporting System (NFIRS) classification

OpenStreetMap basemap for context

## Key Insights

EMS and rescue incidents represented the highest overall density, concentrated in central and south Kent, reflecting demand in high-population areas.
Structure fires and fire/hazmat incidents displayed more localized clustering, highlighting industrial and mixed-use zones as higher-risk areas.
Service calls and non-emergency responses showed a broader spatial distribution, emphasizing routine but resource-intensive activities across residential areas.
Contour mapping proved effective in generalizing dense incident point data into clear risk surfaces, making spatial patterns more interpretable for resource planning.
The project demonstrates how incident density modeling can guide fire departments in positioning apparatus, personnel, and other emergency resources strategically.
