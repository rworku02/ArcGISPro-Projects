# Providing Equitable Public Transit for a Greater Seattle Report
Rahel Worku

After conducting spatial analysis of Seattle’s public transportation accessibility, the
gaps in transit service access were highlighted. Examining the GTFS data and the ACS 2023
5-year estimates show the existing transit stops in Seattle and the areas within a 10-minute
walking radius around those stops. This highlights the regions in need for improvements to
public transportation access. Below are map figures visualizing the inequalities to urge
Seattle policymakers to address the improvements with the current transit system. High
poverty areas such as South Seattle, Renton, and some parts of Shoreline show a lack of
transit stop access, and this aligns over household with no vehicle access. A lack of
reliable transportation adds barriers to residents for job access, education, or healthcare
access. This issue is especially hurting low-income residents because of how restricted
their mobility is compared to the rest of Seattle residents.

We need to expand transit coverage in the high poverty areas that also have little to
no vehicle access. This current coverage is hindering the parts of the population in poverty
who can’t use the current transit to their liking and is potentially holding them back from
necessary travels. Seattle must invest in expanding transit lines and routes to increase
service in the underserved areas of Seattle. Adding more bus stops to the areas outside of
the 10-minute criteria is necessary to bridging that gap for low-income residents.
Policymakers and urban planners must start initiatives to enhance the current public
transit services we oRer to make sure everyone is provided with those same benefits.
