# De-identifying Health Data for Visualization and Sharing


This project focused on the ethical and technical challenges of working with sensitive health datasets. Using ArcGIS Pro, I applied multiple de-identification techniques to childhood lead poisoning case data to ensure confidentiality while still producing meaningful visualizations and insights.


The goal was to gain hands-on experience with HIPAA-compliant data handling and to balance privacy with analytical utility. The project applied different methods to visualize health outcomes while minimizing the risk of re-identification.

## Skills Gained

- Data privacy in GIS: Safe Harbor and suppression rules, HIPAA considerations

- Spatial de-identification techniques: heat maps, hot spot analysis, clustering, tessellation, coordinate rounding

- Aggregation strategies: summarization by ZIP Code, multi-year aggregation, and merging geographies

- Cartographic design for confidentiality: creating visualizations that communicate risk patterns without exposing individual cases

## Tools & Data

- ArcGIS Pro (Spatial Analyst, Hot Spot Analysis, Tessellation tools)

- Public health dataset (childhood lead poisoning case data, de-identified)

- Legal framework: HIPAA Privacy Rule

## Key Insights

Heat maps and clustering were effective for public-facing communication while masking precise case locations.
Hot spot analysis and tessellation allowed for analytical insights without exposing small cell counts.
ZIP Code and year aggregation ensured compliance but introduced trade-offs in spatial and temporal detail.
A multi-method approach is essential — no single technique fully protects confidentiality while preserving analytic value.
